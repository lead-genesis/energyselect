Everything You Need to Know

Can't find your answer? Reach out to us anytime.

After the federal STC discount, a quality 6.6kW solar system typically costs $4,500–$7,500 installed. A larger 10kW system ranges from $7,000–$11,000. Adding a battery (e.g., Tesla Powerwall 2 at 13.5kWh) adds $10,000–$14,000 — the federal rebate slashes the price by 30% or up to $4,200 in this case, bringing the price down significantly. That makes the total price of an average system post rebate around $15,000.

The federal government's STCs provide $1,500–$2,600 off your solar system cost. The Federal Home Batteries Program offers a 30% upfront discount on batteries (~$4,000–$8,000). Victoria offers up to $1,400 for solar installations. NSW offers a $1,500 VPP battery incentive. The federal battery rebate changes from 1 May 2026 — acting sooner means bigger savings.

Most solar battery systems pay for themselves in 5–8 years, depending on your energy usage, system size, and available rebates. After that, you're essentially getting free electricity for the remaining 15–20+ years of your system's life.

Batteries boost your energy self-consumption from 30–40% to 70–99%. With battery prices dropping ~10% yearly and feed-in tariffs as low as 1.5c/kWh in some states, storing your own solar makes more financial sense than ever.

Solar panels still generate 30–70% capacity on cloudy days. At night, you draw from your battery or the grid at reduced rates thanks to daytime feed-in credits. Your system stays grid-connected, so you always have power.
